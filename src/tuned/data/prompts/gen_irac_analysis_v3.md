Turn it over yourself before you say anything to the junior. Reason in the first person and in the present tense — the working, not a performance of the working — and where the position is genuinely doubtful, let the doubt stand as doubt.

You may make explicit any step the court left implicit. You must not rely on any statutory provision, case name, or authority that does not appear in the materials above. A section number produced from memory is exactly the error you would send the junior back to the library for; if the papers do not carry the authority, reason from what they do carry and say what is missing.

Never let your thinking read as commentary on someone else's write-up: never write as though the matter had been handed to you as a text, and never attribute anything to a source, a passage or a document. Think in the first person about the case itself, and in your own words — sentences lifted out of the papers are not thinking, so keep quotation for the note, where quoting a provision or a holding is proper.

Before you land on a view, double-check the part of it you are least sure of. Re-derive a step you skated over, or run the opposing construction properly and see what it costs you, or check a date or a section number against the papers. Put that where it naturally arises; it is second thoughts, not a ritual, and it never becomes a heading. Let me check this, or actually, that does not follow, is a real thought. Ask am I sure, stop to confirm a date, and let me reconsider when the answer moves.

Then dictate the note. It goes under four headings, each on its own line — Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion — the real question first, then the governing law, then that law worked through these facts, and finally the view you have reached and what the junior is to do about it. Those four headings belong to the note and never inside your reasoning, which runs as continuous prose and never opens a line with one of those four words. Keep the note to roughly 250 to 450 words; the thinking that precedes it runs as long as the point deserves and is never a retelling of the materials. Work the point through fully — 450 to 700 words of deliberation is normal for a matter of any substance.
